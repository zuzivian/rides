# Rides

Rides turns your own rideshare history into stats, a map of every trip, and a share card. It runs entirely in your browser: you load your Uber or Lyft data export, and nothing leaves your device.

This is a web prototype. The plan is to turn it into an iPhone app later (see [docs/ROADMAP.md](docs/ROADMAP.md)).

## What you get

After you load a file, Rides plays a **story**: about 12 full-screen cards, each with one big idea and one visual. You get distance drawn around a globe, days in the back seat as filling dials, your most loyal route glowing on the map, the city going dark except for your late-night rides, your busiest month as a calendar, and your **rider type** (for example "The Night Owl Commuter", worked out from your habits). It ends on your share card. Then you land on the **map**, with the stats laid over it and visual highlights below.


- **Totals:** rides, distance, hours in cars, money spent (each currency kept separate)
- **Records:** longest, most expensive and cheapest rides
- **Places and routes:** pickup and dropoff points grouped into places ("Home", "Work", "SFO" and so on), which you can rename. Places get the most specific real name from OpenStreetMap data near them: a shop or venue ("Whole Foods Market", or "near Whole Foods Market"), a street address ("1200 9th Avenue"), an intersection, or the neighbourhood ("Inner Sunset"). Your export's street name is used when the map has nothing more specific. Only where the map has no name at all does a place get one from when you ride there and how far it is from Home, such as "Late nights · 2.5 mi E of Home". Names are worked out on your device. Your top routes, such as "Home → Work, 44 times"
- **Patterns:** a time-of-day × weekday heatmap, late-night rides (00:00–05:00), your busiest and quietest months, and streaks
- **Trends:** a year-over-year comparison, price per mile over time, and your product mix (UberX, Comfort, XL and so on)
- **Travel:** airport runs, cities visited, and average wait for pickup when your export includes request times
- **A map** of every ride as an arc (brighter for routes you ride more), a replay of your year with a date ticker, and tap-a-place details
- **A share card**: a 1080×1920 "my year in rides" PNG with totals and a mini map, drawn on your device. Names and addresses are left out unless you turn on place nicknames

All stats count **rides on your account**. Rides that friends booked for you aren't in your export, so they aren't counted.

## Privacy model

Privacy is the point of this app.

- **No server.** The app is static files. Parsing and stats run in your browser tab.
- **No uploads, no analytics, no cookies.** A Content-Security-Policy in `index.html` blocks network requests to anything except the app's own files and the two basemap hosts below.
- **Map tiles, and nothing else.** Under the ride arcs the map shows an OpenFreeMap dark street map. To draw it, the browser loads MapLibre GL 4.7.1 from unpkg.com and map tiles from tiles.openfreemap.org. The tile host sees which map areas are viewed (and your IP address, as any website would), never ride data. Arcs, places and stats are drawn on your device and never leave it. No ride coordinates are put in any URL, and requests are sent with no referrer.
- **Works without them.** If either host is blocked or offline, the map falls back to a canvas drawn from your rides alone, with no network requests at all. Add `?nobasemap` to the URL to always use that fallback. A line under the map says whether the street map is on, and if not, why (for example "MapLibre script blocked or failed to load").
- **Attribution.** The map shows the credit OpenFreeMap and OpenStreetMap require: © OpenFreeMap © OpenMapTiles © OpenStreetMap contributors.
- **Place names come from OpenFreeMap map tiles for the areas you ride in; your rides never leave your device.** After you load your rides, the browser fetches the z14 map tile (about 2 km across) around each of your places and reads the names in it on your device. Each request carries only tile numbers (`/14/x/y.pbf`), with no coordinates, times or ride data, and goes only to tiles.openfreemap.org. The tile host can still tell, along with your IP address, which tiles you asked for, and so roughly which areas you ride in, including near Home and Work. Nat chose this on 26 Nov 2026: "just show the exact places". Add `?nobasemap` to the URL to turn it off together with the street map.
- **Nothing is stored** except optional place nicknames and the place names read from map tiles, which are saved in your browser's `localStorage`. Tile names are stored as a name, its kind and a zoom level per place, keyed by the same place id as nicknames, so repeat visits don't fetch the tiles again. "Reset names" in the Places card clears both.
- **Never commit real data.** The repo ships a synthetic dataset (`data/sample-trips.csv`, generated by `scripts/gen-sample.mjs`). `private/` is git-ignored so you can keep your own export there for local testing.

## Getting your data

### Uber

1. Go to Uber's Privacy Center (privacy.uber.com) and sign in.
2. Choose **Download your data** and request it. Uber emails you when it's ready, which usually takes a few hours to a couple of days.
3. Unzip the download. Your trips are in `Rider/trips_data.csv` (the name varies a little between export versions).
4. Load that CSV into Rides.

### Lyft

1. In the Lyft app or on lyft.com, open your privacy settings and choose **Download your data**.
2. When the email arrives, download the archive and find the ride history CSV.
3. Load it into Rides.

### Receipt emails

You can also load Uber or Lyft receipt emails saved as `.eml` files, or a whole `.mbox` export (for example, Google Takeout of a "Rides" label). They're parsed in the browser. Only trip times, addresses, distance, fare and ride type are kept. Names and card digits are ignored. Other emails in an mbox are skipped. Receipts don't include coordinates, so receipt-only rides count in the stats but don't appear on the map. Load them together with your export and duplicates are dropped.

Column names differ between export versions. Rides detects columns by matching many known header names. If it can't find a time column, it shows the headers it found, so you can tell what went wrong. Canceled trips, Uber Eats orders and other non-ride rows are skipped and counted.

## Running it

No install step is needed. Node 20 or later is only used for the dev server, the tests and the build check.

```sh
npm run dev      # http://localhost:5173
npm test         # unit tests on the sample data
npm run build    # copy the app into dist/ and check every reference resolves
```

See [AGENTS.md](AGENTS.md) for the full developer guide.
